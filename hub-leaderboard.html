How the score works

Every measure is judged against a fixed standard, never against the other hubs. A hub scores 100 on a measure when it reaches the cut-off, 0 when it reaches the cut-off, and a straight line in between. A hub that is better than all the others but still short of the standard is not called good.

  • Operations (70% of the overall score): OOS % of fleet (the average over the period) · aged OOS bikes (over 7 days) per 100 bikes (today’s snapshot, so only when the period includes today) · OOS repairs finished within 48 hours · OOS clearing rate (OOS repairs finished as a % of new OOS bikes, meaning rider, exchange and recovery arrivals; 100% means the OOS pool is not growing, above 100% means it is shrinking; needs 30+ arrivals) · labour hours per technician per day (logged hours, real technicians with 3+ working days, FREELANCER excluded).
  • Control & quality (30%): MSL parts short (today’s stock, so only when the period includes today) · customer rating (needs 20+ ratings) · damage fee charged: the share of the fee that should have been charged (the chargeable cost, capped at ₹3,000 per job card) that was actually raised, as in the Damage Leakage dashboard, Manual JCs excluded (needs 10+ damage job cards) · latest audit score (the latest audit on or before the end of the period).

  • Core measures are OOS %, aged OOS and repairs within 48 hours. A core measure rated stops a hub from being Good.
  • Missing data is never counted as zero. A hub is rated only if it has enough operations measures, and gets a control & quality score only if it has enough of those.
  • Fleet measures (OOS %, aged OOS) are as of now. The others cover the dates selected above.
  • Comparison set: the main service hubs only. The plant, pilot, test and very small sites are left out, and partner hubs count under their parent hub.
